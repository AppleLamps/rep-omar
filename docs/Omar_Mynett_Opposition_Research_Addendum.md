## 1. Sworn bankruptcy testimony directly contradicts Omar's $5M–$25M disclosure (HIGHEST LEVERAGE)

This is the single most damaging item that has emerged since the original December 2025 report. It converts the disclosure dispute from a "valuation disagreement" into a sworn-statement conflict. [confirmed across sources]

On November 21–26, 2024, William Hailer ("co-founder and self-described CEO" of Rose Lake Capital) testified under oath in U.S. Bankruptcy Court for the District of Delaware in litigation around the Byju's case. Plaintiff's attorney Nicholas Mozal cross-examined him about Rose Lake. The transcript exchange:

- "Does Rose Lake have assets under management?" — Hailer: "Under a legal term of art, I think the answer is no, yeah."
- "Does Rose Lake have capital?" — Hailer: "Rose Lake has a small set of equity positions in a handful of companies, but nothing that are assets that we control through management."
- Asked for approximate value: "I would say de minimus in sort of ownership controls or investments."
- Hailer then agreed the value was under $1 million, and "couldn't tell you" whether it was less than $500,000.
- Asked the largest investment Rose Lake ever made: "we haven't made financial investments with our own capital, so the answer would be zero."
- Largest deal Rose Lake ever made: "less than $10 million."
- He described eStCru as a wine company "in the process of being shut down."

That sworn testimony is dated five months after Omar's 2024 disclosure listed Rose Lake at $5M–$25M and eStCru at $1M–$5M. The two cannot both be true. Hailer was either lying to a federal bankruptcy judge or to his cross-examiner, or Rep. Omar's disclosure was materially false. The National Legal and Policy Center cited this exact testimony when it filed a formal complaint with the Office of Congressional Conduct on Feb 16, 2026.

Offensive use: this is the cornerstone fact for any opposition framing. It is sworn, on the record, in federal court, and predates the controversy. Pair it with Omar's signed disclosure for a clean, side-by-side document attack.

## 4. The confession of judgment… $1.2 million still owed to South Dakota cannabis investors

In April 2023, three Mynett/Hailer-controlled entities (eSt Ventures, Badlands Fund GP, and Badlands Ventures) settled a fraud and breach-of-contract suit brought by three South Dakota cannabis entrepreneurs for $1.7 million. They paid only $500,000. In fall 2023 Hailer signed a confession of judgment admitting the companies still owe $1.2 million. The plaintiffs are now suing Hailer and the companies in Nebraska to collect.

Hailer's email-trail explanation for nonpayment included: "the bank forgetting a zero" on a wire (the entrepreneurs received $50,000 instead of $500,000), references to OFAC holds (a federal agency that enforces sanctions), and a hospital trip. None of it produced the money.

Offensive use: this is documentary evidence that Mynett's business orbit issued false payment promises and signed a sworn admission of $1.2M unpaid debt… while Omar concurrently reported the broader Rose Lake / eStCru complex at $6M–$30M.

## 5. Africa solar panels: the substantive business behind the Kenya/Somalia/UAE travel demand

The original report's foreign-policy conflict-of-interest concern was scaffolded around Ivanhoe Atlantic. The Comer Feb. 5 letter pulled in a separate dimension when it demanded all records of travel by anyone affiliated with Mynett's companies to the UAE, Somalia, or Kenya.

The NY Post connected the dots on Feb. 14, 2026: Rose Lake Capital was actively pitching a solar-panel build-out in Africa. That is the substantive business reason Comer wants the travel records, and it positions Mynett's firm in direct adjacency to active UAE-financed solar infrastructure projects in Somaliland and the Horn of Africa (Abu Dhabi-based Global South Utilities, Abu Dhabi Fund for Development, etc.).

Why this matters for opposition framing:
- It explains the foreign-travel demand as more than a fishing expedition.
- It puts a sitting Member of the House Foreign Affairs Committee in proximity (via spouse) to commercial dealings in jurisdictions where her congressional work touches sanctions, Somali aid, and Gulf diplomacy.
- The Somaliland angle is especially live because Somaliland recognition is itself a contested foreign-policy question in DC.

## 6. House Ethics Committee referral (March 2026) — escalation milestone

After the Feb. 5 Oversight letter, Mynett did not comply with the records request. In early March 2026, Comer's committee formally referred the matter to the House Ethics Committee. This is a meaningful procedural escalation: the Ethics Committee is bipartisan (5 Republicans, 5 Democrats), it has actual authority over Members, and prior referrals have produced resignations (Matt Gaetz being the most recent example cited in the coverage).

Two important caveats for opposition framing:
- The Ethics Committee can vote to ignore the referral. Because it is bipartisan, partisan blockage is plausible.
- Omar's office has called the matter a "political stunt" and "baseless, headline-generating speculation."

Even if the committee declines, the public referral itself is usable: a committee chair has formally asked for an Ethics review of a sitting Member's spousal disclosures in connection with a foreign mining and infrastructure venture.
